The strongest case against

The retailers own the supply, set the rates and already pay creators directly. Every layer you add sits on a revocable licence. Your margin is 1 to 3% of order value after creators are paid. The closest precedents sold cheap or shut.

The idea in one picture

Three layers on top of other people's shops

You do not hold stock or take payment. You send shoppers to the retailer and earn a share of the sale. Each layer depends on what the retailer lets you show.

Claim scorecard

Eight claims in the pitch, tested one by one

None holds up as stated. Two survive with heavy changes. The rest need a test or fail.

Scraping is not legal, so use official feeds
Needs rework

Feeds are safer, but scraping is grey, not illegal per se. No Indian court has squarely ruled; OLX v Padawan (2016) was ex parte Delhi HC orderCO. Feeds exist only for Flipkart and Amazon, and their licences are narrower than the product.

Cross-retailer search for "240 gsm" tees
Niche wedge only

The gap is real: retailer assistants search only their own catalogue. But no feed confirms GSM or fit fields, there is no GSM query dataND, and Google AI Mode already searches across retailers.

Personalisation shows what the user wants
Unproven

All lift evidence is company or vendor claimsCO. No independent Indian fashion study foundND. Test it.

Wardrobes work like wishlists and playlists
High risk

No Indian data on sharing or revisitsND. Whering claims 10M cumulative users with no retention data. Virtual Wardrobe closed in Oct 2026. Polyvore shut in 2018. Weak as a retention engine.

Influencer wardrobes across sites
Best of the three

Demand is real. Supply is contested by Myntra at 8 to 12%, Amazon, Meesho, Instagram, Wishlink and EarnKaro. No retailer documents a sub-publisher revenue shareND. Works only creator-first and in one niche.

Affiliate revenue funds the business
Only if small

Rs 31 to 96 per order, paid 60 to 150 days laterDERIVED. Works only as a low-cost, organic, small-team business.

Add quick commerce later
Later

Possible, but delivery makes you a marketplace with new duties, gig fees and FDI limits. Use wardrobes as a demand sensor first. The earlier report's unknowns still apply Earlier report.

seats.movie proves it
Not evidence

No catalogue, no commission, unverified traffic, 6 days old.

Legal and compliance

Two deadlines land before you could scale

These are facts and citations, not legal advice. Items marked counsel need a written opinion from an Indian lawyer.

E-commerce amendment rules

44days to 1 Jan 2027

Notified 9 Sep 2026 PIBCO Cyril AmarchandEST

  • Disclose ranking parameters in order of weight. Label sponsored listings.
  • "Prior price" must be the lowest price in the last 30 days. Feed MRP shown as-is could breach this.
  • Annual dark-pattern self-audit with a displayed certificate.
  • Whether a link-out platform is an "e-commerce entity" is unclear. Counsel.

DPDP core duties

176days to 13 May 2027

Notice, consent, breach, children and penalties start Mono calendarEST

  • Under-18s cannot be tracked or ad-targeted, even with parental consent (s.9(3)) dpdprules.orgEST. Age-gate, or switch off teen personalisation.
  • Body measurements, skin tone and photos: collect the minimum. No face data unless essential. Counsel.
  • Penalty ceilings up to Rs 250 cr for security failures.

Influencer disclosure

  • ASCI makes both the advertiser and the influencer responsible for an upfront "Affiliate" or "Ad" label ASCICO. The platform that pays influencers is in scope.
  • CCPA fines are small so far: Rs 2.57 cr across 78 cases to 30 Jan 2026 Lok Sabha replyCO. Labels are still required.
  • Put an unremovable "earns commission" label on every public wardrobe and shared link preview.

Images and stored data

  • Flipkart images: resize only Flipkart T&CCO. Amazon: no image caching, no sublicensing Amazon agreementCO.
  • Do not build embeddings on retailer images without written permission.
  • Persistent wardrobes must re-fetch by product ID and drop delisted items. Shared wardrobes may count as sublicensing, which Amazon bars. Counsel.

Countdown counts from today in your browser. As of 18 Nov 2026 the figures are 44 and 176 days. Some advisers count DPDP from 14 May 2027.

What to do instead

A 6-week creator-first wardrobe test

Lead with 10 to 20 creators in one niche, for example premium basics and streetwear tees for GSM-literate shoppers. Their audience brings the traffic. Links run through Cuelinks, EarnKaro, INRDeals, Flipkart and Amazon sub-IDs, so you need no feed deals. The catalogue is a few hundred to a few thousand curated, LLM-enriched products.

Schedule

Wk 1Wk 2Wk 3Wk 4Wk 5Wk 6
1. Access: written permissions
2. Creator wardrobes: 10 to 20 creators
3. Retention: 200 to 500 invited users
4. Search wedge: 500 enriched tees
5. "Need it today" taps

1. Access test, weeks 1 to 2

Apply to the Flipkart API and Amazon Associates. Ask Cuelinks, Admitad and INRDeals in writing for product feeds, sub-ID revenue share and aggregator use. Email partnerships at Myntra, AJIO, Nykaa Fashion and Tata CLiQ. Ask D2C brands such as Souled Store, Snitch, Newme and Bewakoof for direct links.

Continue if at least 3 retailers permit price and image display in a curated wardrobe, in writingPROPOSED

2. Creator test, weeks 2 to 6

Each creator builds wardrobes of 20 to 40 items. Measure opens, saves, shares, outbound click rate, earnings per click and approval.

Continue if earnings per click reach Rs 3 (about 3 times the observed Rs 1) and approval reaches 60%PROPOSED

3. Retention test

Invite 200 to 500 users to build their own wardrobes.

Continue if at least 15% return within 30 days. Shopping apps keep 2.4 to 6.5%, so 15% is a strong signalPROPOSED

4. Search wedge test

Hand-enrich 500 tees. Compare time to find a match against Myntra search with 20 users.

Continue if users find a match 2 times fasterPROPOSED

5. Demand sensor for the earlier quick-commerce idea

Add a "need it today" toggle. Log taps only. Do not fulfil. This gives the first real number for the earlier report's biggest unknown, urgent fashion demand, at near zero cost. Quick commerce carries only about 1% of online lifestyle sales Bain 2026EST. Delivery later brings marketplace duties, the Karnataka 1% gig fee The HinduMEDIA and FDI limits.

Kill the idea if two of these happenPROPOSED

  • Fewer than 3 retailers give written display permission.
  • Earnings per click stay under Rs 2.
  • Fewer than 50% of creators are still active after 30 days.
  • Fewer than 8% of users return to their wardrobe within 30 days.

Budget: founders unpaid. Infrastructure, domains, LLM enrichment and one counsel opinion, capped at Rs 2 to 3 lakhASSUMPTION. I found no sourced figure for this budget.
