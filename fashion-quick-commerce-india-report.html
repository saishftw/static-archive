Fashion quick commerce in India: should you build the store-fulfilled aggregator?

A store-fulfilled, zero-inventory aggregator compared with the dark-store model: market size, 20 players, unit economics you can change yourself, the risks, two scaling paths and a test plan that costs under ₹6 lakh.

FX used: ₹88 = US$1 (assumption, not verified)Tags: R company-reported M media I independent/analyst firm E my estimate N not disclosed

01Executive summary

Verdict

Run a 4-week concierge test. Do not build the app or sign leases yet.

Demand for fast fashion is real but small, and the model you describe is already being copied by well-funded players. The economics work only if stores accept ~20% commission and customers pay most of the delivery cost. Nobody publishes acceptance or stock-accuracy data for broadcast dispatch in fashion, so that is the first thing to measure. The test costs about ₹4–6 lakh E and can kill the idea in a month.

Seven findings

  1. The market is real, tiny and growing fast. Quick commerce carries about 1% of online fashion, against about 70% of online grocery.[2][5] Redseer expects 3–5% of online fashion by 2030, which is roughly $1.4–2.7bn on its own projection ($46–53bn online fashion).[4]

  2. Your model already exists and is being funded. AJIO Rush is store-fulfilled at national scale.[8] ZILO, the best-funded dark-store player, now connects 150+ brand stores and aims for 250.[16] Booon runs a zero-dark-store 2-hour aggregator in six cities.[32] Clozzet and Slazzy are tiny regional versions.[48][49]

  3. Dark stores are burning cash and have a graveyard. Rapid-fashion start-ups burned about $3M in March 2026 alone.[18] Blip shut within roughly a year of launch.[38] Dunzo, a hyperlocal pioneer, was written off by Reliance at $200M.[37]

  4. Unit economics are thin on both sides. At 20% commission, ₹80 delivery fully passed on, 20% returns and ₹120 acquisition cost per order, the store-fulfilled model earns about ₹30 per order before cluster costs, so a ₹6 lakh a month cluster needs ~670 orders a day to break even. The dark store earns ~₹86 per order before store costs and needs ~310 orders a day per store. Both numbers swing hard on delivery recovery and returns (§4).

  5. Commission is the weak point. Reported marketplace rates run 17–25% on Myntra[24] and 14–18% on Flipkart until it cut fashion commission to zero in July 2026.[23] Local stores have little reason to pay 20% plus handle returns for volume they can already sell at the counter.

  6. The real white space is unorganised multi-brand retail, Tier-2 cities and occasion wear. About 45% of fashion GMV is still unorganised.[4] Incumbent store networks are brand-owned outlets, not independents. Dark-store players cluster in Mumbai, Bengaluru and Delhi NCR (§2 coverage chart).

  7. The strongest case against you: the winner is probably whoever owns the stores (Reliance, Myntra's brand ties, NEWME, Snitch) and owns the customer. An aggregator in the middle has no inventory control, no data on stock, and a customer who can walk into the shop next time.

Three biggest unknowns

1 · Broadcast acceptance and fill rate

What share of orders does at least one nearby store accept within 3 minutes and then actually pack in the right size? No public fashion figure exists. Test first.

2 · Will customers pay the delivery fee and wait 60–120 minutes?

43% of quick-commerce users say they do not want to pay any extra fee.[40] Your model assumes they pay all of it.

3 · Will independent stores pay ~20% and stay honest on price?

Mall and brand outlets face lease and MRP limits. Independents may accept but with discounting and walk-in conflicts. No public data on this.

What the table says

Speed versus reach is the real trade-off. Everyone promising 60 minutes has fewer than ~160 nodes; the only player past 1,000 nodes (AJIO Rush) promises 2–4 hours.

Hybrids are winning the argument. ZILO started as dark stores and added brand-store tie-ups to widen catalogue without stock.[16][45] NEWME and Snitch use their own stores as hubs and stay asset-light.[19][30]

Quickeee is not store-fulfilled. Its launch release says it is powered by hyperlocal dark stores.[31]

Horizontals will not go deep. Blinkit, Zepto and Instamart add fashion to lift basket size; fashion is 8–10% of quick-commerce orders in early 2026 in metros.[20] Amazon Now plans 300 cities[36]; Flipkart Minutes lists 77,000+ products across 1,400+ categories in 150+ cities.[33]

Delivery price benchmarks

Borzo: flat ₹35 + ₹8 per km (Sep 2026 help page)[26]; Shadowfax: ₹50 up to 4 km, ₹10 per km after, 7 km maximum (reseller page, undated)[27]; Porter bike from ₹48 incl. 1 km (Bengaluru)[28]. Typical 3–5 km hop: ₹55–85 one way E. Shadowfax charges 25% if a merchant cancels after the rider arrives and 100% after pickup.[27]

Assumptions behind the calculator

  • A revenue = AOV × commission × (1 − return rate). A returned item earns no commission.
  • A delivery: you pay one outbound leg; the customer repays the "passed to customer" share. Returns cost one extra leg plus ₹20 handling.
  • A fixed cost: ₹6 lakh a month per micro-cluster (ops lead, store-success, support) E.
  • Shared per-order costs: payment 2% of AOV[25], packing ₹15, support/tech ₹25 E; acquisition per order set by slider (NEWME's monthly CAC of ₹300–400 per user, spread over ~3 orders, gives ~₹120[19] E).
  • B revenue = AOV × (1 − return rate) × gross margin; markdown and dead stock = 6% of kept sales E.
  • B delivery costs 75% of A's per-leg cost (shorter radius) and the customer pays a flat ₹25 (Slikk's fee[21]).
  • B variable: pick-and-pack ₹20 plus the shared costs above.
  • B fixed cost: ₹8 lakh a month per store (rent ₹2.5L, staff ₹4L, utilities and tech ₹1.5L) E. Grocery dark-store guides quote ₹2–5 lakh rent in Mumbai and ₹25–80 lakh capex.[29]
  • No grocery benchmark applies directly: Redseer's breakeven of 1,200–1,250 orders a day per store is for ₹460 baskets.[6]
Sanity check on Slikk's claim. It targets ₹90–100 crore monthly GMV from about 12 Bengaluru stores.[9] That implies ₹7.5–8.3 crore per store per month, or 1,000–1,800 orders a store a day at an AOV of ₹1,500–2,500. That is far above my 300-order breakeven and above what fashion dark stores normally report, so I treat it as a target, not a result. (My arithmetic, E.)

Store-fulfilled risks: what the evidence says

Stock accuracy and cancellations

No public acceptance, stock-out or cancellation figures exist for fashion store-fulfilment. Fynd describes the core failure: one shirt in one store, two orders seconds apart, both accepted, oversell.[1] A Fynd customer story on Raymond (seen only as a search snippet) says "product not reflecting" had become normal before store systems were unified. For ONDC, one seller guide suggests a cancellation rate under 3% is "good", but that is an advice benchmark, not measured data.[43] Not disclosed N; test it.

Does ping-and-confirm work?

Brand-store routing is exactly how AJIO Rush and ZILO's mall-to-home model work, but both integrate store stock systems, which you lack. ZILO routes an item not in dark-store stock to a brand boutique and delivers in about 120 minutes, so confirmation latency is already a 60 → 120 minute trade.[44][45] No acceptance-rate or confirmation-time data from Swiggy, Zomato, Dunzo, ONDC or Magicpin was found for this report N.

Store willingness, MRP parity and brand-outlet limits

ZILO took until early August 2026 to integrate 150 brand stores and plans 250 by mid-September, which shows brand-store onboarding is slow but possible.[16] Lease, franchise and MRP clauses for mall brand outlets were not found in public sources N. Flipkart cutting fashion commission to zero shows how hard it is to charge sellers.[23]

Store hours, pack time and walk-in conflict

M-Now's dark stores deliver from 6:05 am to 11:45 pm.[7] NEWME runs 24×7 Zip from its own stock.[19] Independent stores usually close around the time impulse demand peaks; no measured store-hours data was found N. Packing time and walk-in priority: not disclosed.

Disintermediation

No fashion-specific evidence found N. In general, food platforms control repeat use through the customer app, loyalty and payments; as an aggregator you can mirror that with prepaid orders and no store phone number shared at checkout. E from general knowledge, not sourced.

Hyperlocal logistics

Shadowfax's own hyperlocal page (search snippet only) claims 98%+ on-time, 100+ cities and 500K+ daily orders, and its developer docs show an API with serviceability and cost check. These are company claims, not independently verified. Borzo and Porter quote live per-order prices (see §4).[26][28] Peak-hour reliability and Tier-2 coverage per provider: not disclosed N; Dunzo's collapse is a reminder that a hyperlocal provider can disappear.[37]

Dark-store risks

Fashion dark stores hold only about 7,000–8,000 styles, so a store can serve "last minute" and little else.[18] Capex and opex for a fashion store were not found; grocery guides quote ₹25–80 lakh and tie break-even to rent below ~5–8% of order value.[29] Sector burn: $2–2.5M in January and $3M in March 2026.[18] Seasonality and dead stock: not disclosed N; my 6% markdown allowance is an assumption.

How the stage numbers were derived

Path 1
  • S0 concierge, 6 weeks: team 2 × 1.5 months × ₹1.2L = ₹3.6L; ~340 orders × (₹80 delivery + ₹120 acquisition) ≈ ₹0.7L; tools ₹0.5L → about ₹4.8L, shown as ₹4–6L. Orders = 10–15 stores × 0.5–1 per day.
  • S1 micro-cluster, 6 months: team 7 × 6 × ₹1.2L = ₹50L; app ₹10L; acquisition 60 orders × 180 days × ₹120 = ₹13L; subsidy ~₹1L → about ₹75L, shown as ₹65–90L. Orders = 40–60 stores × ~1 per day.
  • S2 multi-cluster, 12 months: team 25 × 12 × ₹1.2L = ₹3.6cr; acquisition 400 orders × 365 × ₹120 = ₹1.75cr; tech ₹0.3cr → ₹5.6cr, shown ₹5–7cr. Orders = 250–400 stores × 1.2–1.5.
  • S3 dark layer, 12 months: 3 hubs × ₹1.0cr (fit-out, deposit, part-consigned inventory, see Path 2) = ₹3cr; hub opex ₹8L × 3 × 12 = ₹2.9cr; 40 added staff × 12 × ₹1.2L = ₹5.8cr → ₹11.7cr, shown ₹10–14cr incremental.
  • S4 own inventory: 4,000 orders/day × ₹1,500 × 65% cost of goods × 90 days of stock = ₹35cr working capital alone.
Path 2
  • D0 one store: fit-out ₹20L + deposit ₹10L; inventory 7,000 styles (the capacity quoted in [18]) × 4 units × ₹500 landed cost = ₹1.4cr, half on consignment = ₹70L; team 12 × 6 × ₹1.2L = ₹86L; tech ₹15L; acquisition 120 orders × 180 × ₹120 = ₹26L → ~₹2.3cr, shown ₹2–3cr. Size-by-colour depth: 7,000 styles × ~4 sizes ≈ 28,000 SKUs.
  • D1 3–4 stores: 3 more stores × ₹1.0cr = ₹3cr; team 30 × 12 × ₹1.2L = ₹4.3cr; acquisition 450 × 365 × ₹120 = ₹2cr → ₹9cr incremental, shown ₹8–10cr.
  • D2 8–12 stores: ~7 more stores ≈ ₹7cr stock and fit-out + team 80 × 12 × ₹1.2L = ₹11.5cr + acquisition ₹5cr → ₹25–35cr incl. losses. Orders = 10 stores × ~200.
  • Cross-check: Slikk has raised about $13.5M (₹119cr at ₹88) for roughly six dark stores in Bengaluru, so an order-of-magnitude ₹10–20cr per store of cumulative funding is plausible, my table sits below that.[9][10]

Thresholds are my proposals, not industry standards. Set them before you run the test, and write them down.

Order of work

Week 0: pick one 3 km micro-cluster in one city (Tier-2 gives you the white space; a metro gives you more demand). Recruit 10–15 stores with a one-page agreement: 15–20% commission, MRP parity, 3-minute response. Weeks 1–4: take orders on WhatsApp with a shared catalogue link and a Google Sheet; broadcast to a store WhatsApp group; book riders by hand on Borzo or Porter. Week 5: read the numbers, decide.
